## 3e. New creative approach (set by Zac, Nov 4, 2026)
Built on four ideas, all four together:
1. **Hook-first short video.** Every video earns the first two seconds with a hook and on-screen text, built to work with sound off.
2. **Owner on camera, raw and real.** Neal and the shop floor. Less polish, more trust.
3. **Proof from real jobs.** Before and after, close detail, real customers. Numbers only when verified.
4. **Education and myth-busting.** Straight answers to what buyers actually search, in short video and carousels.
What this replaces: the generic luxury look and the borrowed taglines. Still to define with Zac: the hook formulas we will test, the graphic templates (a small set, reused), and how organic posts get picked for ads.

## 3f. Video system (set Nov 4, 2026)
- **Types:** owner answers (30 to 45 sec, one question, one take); B-roll with text (15 to 25 sec); proof reel (15 to 25 sec, result first); mistakes and myths (30 to 40 sec).
- **Every video:** the first frame is the hook, big text plus action inside two seconds. No logo intro, no music swell. Built for sound off: burned-in captions, 8 words or fewer per card, inside phone-safe zones. New shot or text change every 2 to 3 seconds. One idea.
- **Weekly color:** text, highlight, and cover frame follow that week's palette so video matches the feed. The cover is the hook frame, tinted by Hiroshi. A separate designed cover only for key videos.
- **Filming:** vertical 9:16, steady, natural light, clip-on mic for Neal, two takes at most, chest up at eye level looking at the lens. Standard B-roll wide, medium, close, 5 to 8 sec each. One marked before and after angle per job. Three alternate first lines per answer. Every upload labeled by calendar slot.
- **Testing:** budget is about $2,000 a month. One test at a time, two variants, 2 to 3 weeks, judged on real leads and booked jobs. Organic is the first test; the best one or two go to ads.

## 4. Compliance and claims (hard rules)
- Never say "rock chip warranty." XPEL's warranty excludes rock chips and road debris.
- Never say 9H, scratch-proof, or give durability numbers. Self-heal clips say "light marring only."
- Do not use marketing figures the XPEL spec sheet does not support: "98% IR rejection," "98% TSER," "SPF 1000."
- **Georgia tint law:** do not state any tint percentage until checked at legis.ga.gov. HB 1022 (2026) may have changed the limits. Current law: 32% on front side windows and on sedan rear windows.
- **Offers:** every offer carries this terms line: valid with a full-vehicle window tint package, one offer per vehicle, cannot be combined, no cash value, mention the code, ends 11:59 PM on the last date. No fake countdowns. No promos on Veterans Day or Thanksgiving.
- **Current promos (approved by Neal):** THANKS, $100 off ceramic coating with a tint package, code THANKS, Nov 9 to 24. BLACK FRIDAY, $150 off PPF or ceramic with tint, code FRIDAY, Nov 27 to 30.
- **Drone footage:** needs documented FAA Part 107 provenance before commercial use.

## 7. What the research says works (labelled by confidence)
- Long-running competitor ads lead with no clear hook, authority, price, offers, and questions. Quote-style calls to action lead. Moderate to low confidence: small sample, hooks hand-coded.
- Price-first ads sit around $99 to $249 for full-car tint. That is race-to-the-bottom and not a model for a premium XPEL shop.
- Talking-head owner videos are not common in the competitor ads seen, which is an opening for Neal. This is a hypothesis, not tested.
- Plan of record: 12 owner-led videos (Georgia tint law, ceramic vs dyed vs carbon, does tint cut heat, what tint costs, install day, 3 tint mistakes, Tesla and EV tint, what XPEL does and does not cover, Meet Neal, Q4 offer, rapid-fire questions, tint with kids and pets).
