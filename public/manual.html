Users Manual

How every part of the Open Heart Media CRM works, what to click, and who it is for. Read the page for your job. It takes five minutes.

Updated November 2026

Start here

  1. Sign in at app.openheartmediaco.com with your own work email and the temporary password Zac sent you privately.
  2. Pick your own password the first time. Use at least 10 characters. Nobody else ever needs it, including Zac.
  3. Look at Home. It is built around you: your work, what is late, your calendar, and your clients.
  4. Forgot it? Ask Zac or Griffin to reset it. You will get a new temporary one and pick your own again.

Basecamp links open Basecamp, which uses your own Basecamp login, so you see what you already have access to there. The CRM never shows Basecamp content to people who could not already see it.

Who sees what: everyone sees Home, Work, Mail, Content, Clients, Onboarding, How We Work and Conversions. The sales pipeline (Outreach) is limited to Lisa, Michelle, Brad, Griffin and Zac, and the server enforces it, so it is not just hidden.

Home

Your morning screen. Open it first.

  • Top cards: your open work, what is late across all clients, unread client mail, active clients, and (for sales) waiting replies. Click a card to jump there.
  • Needs attention: the short list of things that are late or waiting on you.
  • Agenda: today and the next days from your calendar.
  • Your clients: one tile per client with open and late counts. Click one to open its page.
  • Refresh pulls new data from Basecamp. If it says Basecamp is not connected, click Connect Basecamp once.

Work

Every open to-do across every client, in one list.

  • Filter to My work to see only what is assigned to you.
  • Late items are marked. Clear those first.
  • Click an item to open it in Basecamp and finish it there. Finishing it in Basecamp is what clears it here.
  • New client emails and tasks are routed to the person for that kind of work (see Who handles what on the Team page).

Mail

  1. Click Connect your Google account once. This links your own work mailbox, not anyone else's.
  2. Client email shows here so replies are not missed. Client replies are due the next business day.
  3. Reply from Gmail as normal. The CRM only reads and organizes.

Content: how a post gets made, start to finish

This is the flow every client follows. The Content page shows where each week stands.

  1. Plan. The monthly content calendar sets what posts on which day for each client. The film calendar shows the next shoots.
  2. Shot list. Before a shoot, the client's Weekly pack tab holds the shot list and timeline. Confirm job addresses with the client first.
  3. Shoot and upload. Upload to the client's Drive folder. Name every file CLIENT_TYPE_DATE_Subject_LABEL_001. Wrong names are the number one reason things get lost.
  4. Due Friday at 5:00 PM. Content for the next week is due every Friday. The Content page shows what was uploaded and what is missing.
  5. Edit and design. Editors and designers work from the uploads. Long pauses, filler words and messy talking-head edits go back to the editor.
  6. Caption. Open the client's Copy tab. Every caption has a hook, a body and a CTA, follows the client's own guidelines and the 16 copy rules, uses American spelling, and has no em dashes. Talking-head and testimonial posts need no topic, just the footage.
  7. Approve. Everything goes to To be approved by Zac in Basecamp. Four clients also need client approval: URC, Nitro Pro, BKVF and CISGA. Their folders are copied to the client approval folder and the right approvers are tagged. Griffin writes URC captions first, then the client approves.
  8. Package to post. Once a post is approved, its caption and its final creative are packaged together: caption, CTA, the media file, the date and the time, all in one place and ready to post. A post missing its caption, its creative or its approval is not packaged and does not go out.

Rule of thumb: if you are unsure whether something is approved, it is not. Ask before posting.

How the content is built: research, formats, angles

Nothing is made from a blank page. Every weekly pack is built from the same stack, in this order.

  1. Research. For each client we read what real customers in that industry complain about (Reddit, reviews, searches) and what competitors run in the Meta Ad Library and Google. Every finding is labeled by how strong the evidence is. A finding is only called proven after our own post or ad test shows it.
  2. The Brain. The research, the client's offers, audience, real customer wording, confirmed facts and compliance rules are stored on the client's Brain tab. The writer reads it before every pack. Facts the client has not confirmed are never posted.
  3. Formats. Each client has its own format file: which post types work for them (Reel, carousel, graphic, talking head, testimonial), how long, and what the hook looks like. The pack follows it. Nothing new is filmed for clients where the archive is the plan.
  4. Angles. Every post is tagged with one awareness level: 1 potential, 2 pain, 3 promise, 4 proof, 5 offer. A week covers different levels and formats, and organic leads with levels 1 to 3.
  5. Triggers. National holidays and client-confirmed promos take the slot they fall on. A promo that is not confirmed in writing is never posted.
  6. The pack. From all of that, each slot gets a hook (18 words or fewer), a shot list or archive pull, on-screen text, and a caption with a hook, a body and a CTA, in the client's voice and the 16 copy rules.
  7. Winners loop. When a post does well, mark it as a winner (see Winners for Ads). That format and angle becomes a proven pattern in the next packs, and a task goes to the ads owner.

What "proven" means here: a format or angle is proven only when one of our own posts or ads won with it. Until then it is a well-researched bet, and the pack treats it that way.

Winners for Ads

Three taps. Open Winners for Ads in the menu.

  1. Pick the client and platform, and paste the link to the post.
  2. Tap why it won: most views, saves, shares, comments, or got leads.
  3. Tap Mark winner. The ads owner gets a task, and the next packs learn from it.

Then, on each winner, tap Using in ads or Not a fit so the team can see what has been tested. Judge winners in ads by real leads (forms and calls of 60 seconds or more), not likes.

Blogs

Every client gets two blog posts a month. Open Blogs, pick the client and click Write a blog post (or leave the topic blank and it picks from the research). Each post comes with a title, keyword, meta description and the full text. Anything that needs checking is listed under Check before publishing. Frank copies it into the site and marks it published.

Alerts and reminders

Admins can send a short alert or reminder to the whole team. It appears at the top of Home. Tap Done when you have handled it.

Shot lists: how to read one and how to film from it

A shot list is the plan for one shoot. It lives on the client's page under Weekly pack, named CLIENT_SHOTLIST_DATE. Write one by picking the shoot date and clicking write. It is also written automatically on the Monday of any week that has a shoot on the film calendar.

The rule that matters most: the numbers are a hard limit. Every shot says exactly how much to film. The shoot total at the top is the most you film. Stop when you reach it. No extras. Fewer, better clips beat a folder of hundreds that someone has to sort through. The shots themselves are creative direction: if you see something better in the moment, film that instead. Just stay inside the count.

What is on a shot list

  • Shoot total. The cap for the whole shoot: at most 40 clips, 25 photos and 6 takes. The CRM counts it for you and trims anything over.
  • Talking-head scripts. Someone from the client speaks to camera. Each script has a hook (the first line, one breath), a short body, and a closing line that tells people what to do next. Each script is 1 take. A take is one recording of the whole script. A second try is a second take. Takes are separate from clips.
  • Testimonials (when they fit). A real customer, patient or student we can ask, plus 3 easy questions that get a natural story. Ask open questions. Never put words in their mouth. 1 take each, counted in the 6 takes.
  • B-roll. Short supporting footage with no one speaking to camera: the work, the product, the people, the place or an event. Each shot is tagged with its type and gets 2 to 4 clips of about 4 to 8 seconds. A clip is one short piece of footage. Get a wide shot, a close-up and a movement shot, then move on.
  • Drone (when it fits). Aerial shots of a site, building or event that looks good from above. 2 clips per shot.
  • Photos. Stills labeled team, headshot, jobsite, product, event or location. 1 to 3 photos per shot.
  • Must-gets. The specific shots a scheduled post needs. Get these first. If you only get one thing done, get these.
  • On set. A short pep line for the videographer, and three easy, fun things that put the client at ease: a simple question to ask the owner, a moment that gets them laughing or talking naturally, and a way to involve them in a shot. Clients relax when they feel like part of it, and relaxed people film better.

On the day

  1. Read the shoot total and the must-gets before you leave.
  2. Film the must-gets first, then talking heads, then b-roll and photos.
  3. Swap any shot for a better one when you see it. The count does not change.
  4. Stop at the total. Upload the same day with the right file names (CLIENT_TYPE_DATE_Subject_LABEL_001).

Why we cap it

Some weeks we were getting hundreds of files from one client. Every extra clip costs the editor time to review and the team time to name and sort. A tight list is faster to shoot, faster to edit and gets posts out sooner. The weekly pack works the same way: each post says how many clips it needs, or says it can be made from footage we already have.

Clients

One page per client. Open it from Clients or from your Home tiles.

  • Overview: open and late to-dos, the linked Basecamp project, Drive folder and key links. If the Basecamp project is not linked, pick it from the list and click Link.
  • Content: what is planned and posted for this client.
  • Weekly pack: the week's shot list and deliverables.
  • Copy: captions, hooks and the client's research bank of real customer language.
  • Brain: who the client is, offers, audience, and the facts that must never be wrong.
  • Formats and Look: the post formats they use and their brand look.
  • Guidelines: the client's own rules, including compliance. Read these before writing anything.

Onboarding

New clients fill in the onboarding form. Each submission lands here with its date, and a Basecamp project is created from it. If a submission shows no project, open it and link or create one. Then assign the first tasks.

Conversions (ads team)

Proof that ads create real leads, not just clicks.

  • Every real conversion is a form submit or a phone call of 60 seconds or more from an ad. Page opens and tap-to-call taps are secondary.
  • A small script on the client's site saves the ad click ID with each lead. When the lead becomes a booked job we send that back to Google (a file upload) and to Meta (a server event) so the ad platforms learn from real jobs.
  • The list is Visiting Nurse, Matchett, Sun & Shield, iFab, ABLS, NABS and Open Heart Media. Forms are tracked first. Call tracking (60 seconds or more) is set up for every client right after.
  • The Conversions tab shows whether the conversion system is live and the setup steps for each client. Until it is live, the checklist there is the work to do.

Outreach (sales team only)

Lisa, Michelle, Brad, Griffin and Zac.

Security, in plain words

  • The address is https only. Never use it on a public computer, and sign out on shared devices.
  • Use a password you use nowhere else. Do not share it. Ask for a reset instead.
  • Client logins, ad accounts and API keys never go in the CRM, chat or email. Ask Zac.
